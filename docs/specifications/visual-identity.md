# OceanMail Visual Identity Direction

Status: **ACTIVE DESIGN DIRECTION — FINAL ARTWORK NOT YET APPROVED**

This document preserves the current organization-level OceanMail brand/logo direction. It is intentionally more specific than a mood board but does **not** declare any generated mockup to be final production artwork.

## Design goals

The OceanMail mark should be:

- simple enough to recognize immediately and remember after brief exposure;
- legible at favicon/app-icon size as well as on a large display or printed surface;
- distinctive by silhouette, not dependent on tiny details;
- understandable in one color, even if the primary treatment uses two colors;
- maritime and communications-oriented without becoming a literal illustration;
- contemporary, trustworthy, and friendly rather than ornate or novelty-driven;
- usable as both a standalone symbol and as part of the `OceanMail` wordmark.

The preferred direction favors symbolic economy: one shape should carry more than one idea where possible instead of stacking separate ocean, boat, globe, envelope, radio, and network icons.

## Current color direction

The current preferred palette is the original OceanMail family used in the latest accepted explorations:

- deep navy / ocean blue;
- bright cyan / teal;
- white or negative space.

Exact production color values have **not** been selected. Do not infer canonical hex/RGB values from AI-generated mockups.

Earlier yellow/orange sunset, green, purple, burgundy, and other exploratory palettes are not current palette decisions.

## Current leading mark

### The `O` is the symbol

The current leading construction uses the standalone OceanMail symbol as the first letter `O` in the `OceanMail` wordmark rather than placing a separate icon above or beside a second literal `O`.

The `O` should:

- read clearly as the letter `O` first;
- be slightly reminiscent of a globe/ocean/world connection without literal latitude/longitude grid lines;
- remain a simple circular or near-circular form with strong negative space;
- integrate a single cyan/teal letter/envelope/sail-like swoosh passing through it;
- preserve a clean navy lower/ocean component only if it remains visually simple;
- avoid secondary details that make the mark read as a face, eyes, lips, or a smile.

The swoosh should feel fast and directional, but not like a generic paper-airplane icon pasted onto a circle. Its value is that it can simultaneously suggest mail/letter, sail, motion, and maritime connection.

### Wordmark integration

The symbol should occupy approximately the same visual height and rhythm as the rest of the wordmark so the result reads as one name, not an icon plus a caption.

Current preferred wordmark direction:

- symbol-as-`O`, followed by `ceanMail`;
- a rounded/geometric contemporary sans or purpose-drawn custom lettering;
- substantial, friendly strokes with good small-size legibility;
- `cean` in deep navy and `Mail` in cyan/teal is the current favored color split;
- avoid narrow/tall display lettering as the default production wordmark unless later testing shows a strong reason to change.

The typography shown in the AI concept boards is not a known canonical font. The favored `#9` treatment was effectively custom/generated lettering. Production work should reproduce the desired proportions and personality rather than searching for an exact nonexistent font name.

## Envelope-derived `M` requirement

The current unresolved refinement is to replace the `M` in `Mail` with an **envelope whose own geometry reads as the letter M**, while preserving the footprint and rhythm of the existing `M`.

This requirement is easy to misread, so the intended geometry is explicit:

- the envelope replaces the `M`; it is not an envelope drawn inside, on top of, or next to a conventional `M`;
- the envelope's outer left and right side edges act as the two vertical stems of the `M`;
- the **bottom edge of the envelope flap** supplies the two inward diagonals / central valley that make the envelope read as `M`;
- the envelope should occupy roughly the same width, cap height, baseline, and stroke weight as the `M` it replaces;
- the word must still read immediately as `OceanMail` at normal and small sizes;
- avoid adding a second chevron, white notch, or decorative inner `M` that makes the symbol merely look like an ordinary `M` with envelope decoration.

The last attempts in the retired design thread did **not** satisfy this requirement and must not be treated as accepted artwork.

## Explicit pitfalls and rejected directions

The following were explored and are not the current direction:

- complex combinations of envelope + large wave + radio/Wi-Fi arcs;
- literal lighthouse, anchor, compass, globe-network, bird, or sailboat illustrations as the primary mark;
- globe grid lines combined with sails when the result becomes too pictorial;
- sail/wave compositions that accidentally resemble two eyes and a smile;
- paired waves that read like lips;
- an `M` with a white chevron/notch added to imply an envelope rather than an envelope whose structure itself forms the `M`;
- exaggerated 1970s bubble/display lettering;
- tall condensed Art Deco lettering as the default final wordmark;
- highly illustrative retro-sunset and Art Deco/Raymond-Loewy-style compositions as final artwork.

The 1970s/1980s sunset-circle, Art Deco, Streamline/Loewy, and earlier wave-envelope explorations remain useful **historical inspiration** for geometry, motion, and confidence, but they were superseded by the simpler integrated mark/wordmark direction.

## Design tests before approval

A final candidate should be checked in at least these forms:

1. full-color horizontal wordmark;
2. one-color black or white;
3. standalone `O` symbol;
4. favicon/app-icon scale;
5. small UI/header scale;
6. print at modest size;
7. light and dark backgrounds.

It should remain recognizable without gradients. Gradients may be used as a treatment later, but they must not be required for the logo to function.

Before public launch, the selected mark/wordmark also needs a trademark/similarity review against materially similar communications, maritime, mail, and technology marks.

## Unresolved work

The visual identity is not finished. Remaining decisions include:

- exact geometry of the simplified `O`/globe/ocean symbol;
- final shape of the mail/letter/sail swoosh;
- successful envelope-derived `M` construction as specified above;
- final typeface versus custom lettering;
- exact spacing, optical alignment, and color values;
- monochrome/reversed variants and clear-space/minimum-size rules;
- final vector masters and export set;
- trademark/similarity review.

Until those are resolved and explicitly approved, generated images from design conversations are references only, not canonical brand assets.
